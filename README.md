# Illustrative Evaluation Framework — SAOR Model

A self-directed, mixed-methods evaluation framework for the **SAOR** (Support, Ask and Assess, Offer Assistance, Refer) brief alcohol intervention model, built to demonstrate an approach to implementation science and outcomes evaluation.

> **This project uses synthetic, illustrative data throughout.** It was built to demonstrate evaluation methodology — a logic model, mixed-methods analysis, and equity-focused thinking — not to represent real patient data or real service findings.

## Why this project exists

This was built as a self-directed portfolio piece ahead of applying for a Research Assistant post evaluating the **SAOR EBI** alcohol early intervention service at the School of Medicine, University College Cork. It's a practical demonstration of how I'd approach a real service evaluation: starting from a logic model, defining process and outcome questions, and analysing both quantitative and qualitative data to answer them.

## The evaluation framework

**Process evaluation questions**
- Are practitioners across Tiers 1–2 using "teachable moments" as intended?
- Is the SAOR conversation delivered flexibly, or does time pressure reduce it to a checklist?
- Are referral pathways from Tier 2 into Tier 3/4 actually cohesive?

**Outcome evaluation questions**
- Do AUDIT scores show a measurable reduction post-intervention?
- Is there a measurable drop in ED re-attendance?
- Are outcomes equitable across subgroups (e.g. urban vs rural)?

The full logic model (inputs → activities → outputs → short/long-term outcomes) and methodology are set out in the [full report](Illustrative%20Evaluation%20Framework.pdf).

## Key findings (illustrative data)

| Metric | Overall | Urban | Rural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean AUDIT score (pre) | 15.2 | 15.8 | 14.5 |
| Mean AUDIT score (post) | 7.3 | 6.2 | 8.8 |
| Mean reduction | -7.9 | -9.5 | -5.7 |
| 6-month ED re-attendance | 23.3% | — | — |

The synthetic data shows a meaningful average reduction in AUDIT scores overall, but a smaller reduction among rural patients — a pattern that, in the qualitative findings, lines up with reported transport and access barriers to follow-up care. This equity gap is explored further in the [full report](Illustrative%20Evaluation%20Framework.pdf).

A short thematic analysis of illustrative practitioner and patient quotes is also included in the report, surfacing themes such as stigma, time and resource constraints, and referral pathway ambiguity — all feeding back into the process evaluation questions above.
